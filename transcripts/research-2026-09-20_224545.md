## Initial source assessment: multimodal AI

Multimodal generation appears feasible, but image and video estimates cannot use one generic “generation” factor.

### Image generation

Bertazzini et al. measured more than 9,000 generations across 17 open diffusion models. The reported median energy per image ranged from approximately `0.000086–0.00408 kWh`, a roughly 47-fold spread. Resolution effects also varied substantially by model, while prompt length was not a significant driver in their experiment. [Bertazzini et al., *The Hidden Cost of an Image*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.17016)

Limitations:

- The study measured open models running on known hardware, not commercial services such as Midjourney or Adobe Firefly.
- It provides inference electricity measurements, not a complete estimate of data-center overhead, hardware manufacture, or water.
- Results should not be presented as exact values for closed commercial models.
- Model choice produces such a wide spread that a single “one AI image” estimate would be misleading.

Provisional decision: **use with qualifications**. The calculator could support broad low–central–high image estimates based on measured open-model results, but should not falsely attach those values to unsupported proprietary models.

### Video generation

EcoLogits now has a documented video estimator using model, resolution, duration, and audio as inputs. It returns interval estimates for electricity, carbon, water consumption, and embodied impacts. [EcoLogits video estimator](https://ecologits.ai/latest/tutorial/video_generation/)

Its method derives generation time from resolution and frame count, maps models to assumed hardware configurations, adds data-center overhead, and propagates hardware-power ranges. Water calculations include modeled on-site cooling and electricity-related water consumption. [EcoLogits video methodology](https://ecologits.ai/latest/methodology/video_generation/)

The underlying 2026 study validated its scaling model across six open video models and three GPU configurations, reporting less than 3% mean absolute percentage error within those tested conditions. It then extrapolated to proprietary systems. [Jegham, Gamazaychikov, and Luccioni, *Lights, Camera, Carbon*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04553)

Limitations:

- Proprietary-model estimates are extrapolations, not direct measurements.
- EcoLogits assigns the entire assumed machine’s power to a request rather than estimating shared or batched use.
- Provider location, PUE, WUE, and hardware are assumptions when request-specific information is unavailable.
- Embodied water is excluded because the methodology lacks sufficient data.
- The underlying paper is recent and should not be treated as settled consensus.

Provisional decision: **use with prominent qualifications**. Video has a stronger ready-made calculation path than image generation, but its ranges and warnings must remain visible.

## Source assessment: project-based AI workload

This feature is supportable, but it should distinguish measured usage from estimated usage.

### Evidence checked

EcoLogits calculates LLM inference impacts primarily from the selected model, generated output tokens, and request latency. Its current methodology does not yet fully account for input-token length, even though it acknowledges that long contexts can materially affect coding and retrieval workloads. It also excludes networking, end-user devices, training, and data collection. [EcoLogits methodology](https://ecologits.ai/latest/methodology/) · [LLM inference method](https://ecologits.ai/dev/methodology/llm_inference/)

This is especially relevant to coding agents: they repeatedly read files, tool results, and conversation history. A 2026 hardware study found that context length, output length, batching, model architecture, and inference phase all affect request energy; simple energy-per-output-token accounting is therefore incomplete. [Vellaisamy et al., *Characterization of Request and Token Energy Costs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28044)

Actual provider records can supply better workload data:

- OpenAI usage records distinguish input, output, cached, and reasoning tokens. [OpenAI usage documentation](https://platform.openai.com/docs/api-reference/batch/object?api-mode=responses)
- Anthropic usage records distinguish uncached input, cache creation, cache reads, output tokens, and tool-related usage. [Anthropic usage documentation](https://docs.anthropic.com/en/docs/about-claude/pricing)

### Important contrary evidence

The original calculator’s 100,000-output-token coding session is a rough benchmark, not evidence that a typical employee session always uses 100,000 tokens. It should not become the default for every project.

Elapsed working time is also a poor primary input. An agent may spend time waiting for the employee, running local tools, or sitting idle; those minutes do not correspond directly to continuous model inference.

Even complete token logs cannot currently produce an exact environmental result:

- EcoLogits models output tokens more directly than input-context processing.
- Proprietary model architecture and serving hardware remain uncertain.
- Cached input may have a different impact from uncached input.
- Tool execution on external systems may fall outside the LLM estimate.
- Request batching and shared infrastructure are generally unknown.

### Provisional decision

**Use with qualifications.** The feature should offer two clearly labeled modes:
